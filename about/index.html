About Roberto

I pay attention to the business, the relationships around it, and the patterns that can be difficult for a leader to see from inside the situation.

My work draws on strategy, organizational leadership, facilitation, negotiation, and the kind of listening that makes hidden assumptions easier to recognize.

I tend to see how the pieces of a complicated situation connect: the strategic question, the people carrying it, and the pattern underneath the latest problem.

I also notice when capable people are using the same words to describe different realities. Strategic disagreement often begins there. Helping people understand what is actually being said—and what remains unspoken—can change the possibilities available to the whole business.

I bring perspective and advice when I have something useful to offer. I do not assume that an outside expert should prescribe the answer. The leader remains closest to the company and responsible for the choice. My job is to help them see enough of the situation to use that responsibility well.

The rooms I have been in

I grew up in board meetings. In some ways, I never left.

My parents built a business, and I grew up inside it: strategy at the dinner table, decisions weighed out loud, and consequences that were personal.

At One Earth Future, a foundation that incubated peacebuilding programs, I spent seven years helping the organization understand what was working and decide what to do next. I built the program-development process that became the foundation's strategic framework, led impact and learning, and worked closely with leadership through a full organizational transition.

I have sat on the founder's side of the table too. I bootstrapped a startup and analyzed early-stage investments for an angel firm. I continue to carry leadership responsibility inside an organization pursuing an ambitious mission. That keeps me close to the practical consequences of the decisions leaders ask other people to carry.

Those experiences taught me how lonely leadership can become, especially when everyone else needs you to project confidence. They also taught me that a strategically sound answer can still fail if it ignores the relationships, commitments, and lived reality of the people who must carry it.

Why Clearly Audacious

Organizations give people a way to make something possible together.

I am drawn to leaders who care about what they are building and the effect it has on the people involved.

Some lead businesses with an explicit social mission. Others are creating a valuable product, meaningful employment, or a company they want to be proud of. I care less about the label than about the seriousness with which they hold the responsibility.

The name Clearly Audacious holds both sides of the work. Audacity names the willingness to imagine and pursue what does not yet exist. Clarity makes that pursuit more honest, grounded, and possible to carry with other people.

Who I work with

Founders and executives willing to look closely at the business—and at their own part in what happens next.

You bring a real question and are willing to examine your own part in it.

You want an independent partner who can hold complexity, challenge your current reading of the situation, and help turn understanding into decisions and action. The relationship is most useful when you have enough authority to act on what becomes clear.

I am based in Boulder, Colorado, and work with leaders across Boulder, Denver, and beyond.
